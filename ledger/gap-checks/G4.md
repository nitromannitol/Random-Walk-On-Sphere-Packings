## Answer

**Theorem 3.3: CONFIRMED. Theorem 3.6: CONFIRMED. Theorem 3.7: CONFIRMED.**

The counterexample satisfies all three printed statements in [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.11673v2). Line numbers below refer to [rw-spheres.tex](paper/rw-spheres.tex).

The possible exclusions fail:

- \(\mathcal D\) is an arbitrary **subset**, with no openness requirement (line 226; standing convention, line 542). The finite square tiling meets local finiteness, convexity, orthogonality, and the requirement that each vertex lie inside its cell. Also, \(\overline U\subset\mathcal D\).
- \(\mathcal V[U]\) consists of vertices inside \(U\) (553–554). Its boundary consists exclusively of **outside vertices joined by an edge** (563–564). An unshared facet creates no boundary vertex. Likewise, \(\mathcal E[\overline U]\) contains existing edges incident to \(\mathcal V[U]\) (556–557).
- Section 3 adds no standing assumption excluding cells touching \(\partial\mathcal D\) (899–906). The subsection containing 3.6–3.7 explicitly covers all dimensions (1123), including \(d=2\).

To specify the grid precisely, take
\[
P_{ij}=[i/n,(i+1)/n]\times[j/n,(j+1)/n],
\quad v_{ij}=((i+\tfrac12)/n,(j+\tfrac12)/n).
\]
For odd \(n\ge3\), the graph boundary of \(\mathcal V[U]\) is exactly the two rows at \(y=1/2,3/2\). Conductances are all \(1\). The proposed \(h_C\) is smooth and harmonic everywhere and vanishes on both rows. Consequently the unique discrete Dirichlet solution is \(h_D\equiv0\).

The definition of \(\widehat U\) (568–573) gives
\[
\widehat U=[0,1]\times[\tfrac12-\tfrac1{2n},\tfrac32+\tfrac1{2n}],
\quad |\widehat U|=1+\tfrac1n,\quad \varepsilon=\sqrt2/n.
\]
Thus all required derivative bounds \(L,M\), including \(M\vee1\), are uniformly bounded. At \(v_n=(1/2,1+1/(2n))\),
\[
|h_D(v_n)-h_C(v_n)|=\cos(\pi/(2n))\longrightarrow1.
\]

Each conclusion nevertheless forces convergence to zero:

- **3.3 — CONFIRMED:** Its hypotheses and definitions are at 1032–1044. Choosing \(k=\varepsilon^{-1/2}\) in the explicit bound (1048) makes its right side \(O(\sqrt\varepsilon)\to0\).
- **3.6 — CONFIRMED:** Choose fixed \(K=3\). Every cell has area \(n^{-2}>\varepsilon^3=2\sqrt2/n^3\), so \(\mathcal A=\varnothing\) (1163–1168). The component term is explicitly zero in this case (1179–1180). The smallness condition eventually holds, and (1174) becomes \(O(\varepsilon\log\varepsilon^{-1})\to0\).
- **3.7 — CONFIRMED:** From the definition of \(Q_e\) (725–728), every grid edge has \(|Q_e|=1/(2n^2)\). Hence
  \[
  \operatorname{diam}(P_v)=\sqrt2/n=2|Q_e|^{1/2},
  \]
  satisfying (1333) with fixed \(F=2,\alpha=1/2\), including side cells. The smallness condition eventually holds, and (1338) tends to zero.

The underlying omission is visible at 850–854: the divergence-theorem calculation omits flux through unshared facets on \(\partial\mathcal D\). The proposed interior-cell hypothesis excludes these cells.
