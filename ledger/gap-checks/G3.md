## Prompt

````text
You are checking a claimed error in a published mathematics paper. Paper: paper/rw-spheres.tex (Bou-Rabee and Gwynne, "Random walk on sphere packings and Delaunay triangulations in arbitrary dimension", arXiv:2405.11673v2). Lemma 5.1 starts at line 1627 (\label{lemma:geometric-constraint}); read its full statement, its proof, and every definition it uses (the cells C_s / P_v, U, S, k, delta, the grid, and the conventions in Sections 1-2).

Claim to test, about part (b) of Lemma 5.1 (the conclusion that cells are contained in balls of radius O(k sqrt d), under the hypothesis that every grid point of the k-grid within distance 2k of U has a point of S within delta k sqrt d of it):

Any grid point g with g_1 >= 1 is at distance >= sqrt(0.99^2 + (d-1)/4) >= 2.056 > 2.01 from s when d >= 14, so all grid points of B_2(U) have g_1 <= 0; the hypothesis holds for every delta; and C_s contains the ray s + t e_1, t >= 0.

Answer with a verdict for each part: CONFIRMED, REFUTED or UNCLEAR, with the decisive reasons and line numbers. Be concise (under 600 words).

## Answer

(i) **CONFIRMED, with a harmless rounding correction.** The printed hypothesis is cube occupancy, which implies the Euclidean distance bound used in your argument ([lines 1636–1639](paper/rw-spheres.tex:1636)).

For \(\delta=0.1\), nearest-grid rounding gives \(\|p-g\|\le k\sqrt d/2\), so
\[
\|g-s\|\le1.1k\sqrt d+0.05k\le1.955256k<2k
\]
for \(d\le3\). Replace the claimed \(1.955k\) bound by \(1.956k\). Since \(s\in U\), this ensures \(g\in B_{2k}(U)\); neither \(p\) nor \(y\) needs to belong to \(U\). The supplied site satisfies
\[
\|s''-y\|
\le\delta k\sqrt d+\tfrac12k\sqrt d+\|y-s\|-t
=\|y-s\|-0.05k,
\]
contradicting the nearest-site definition of \(\mathcal C_s\) ([lines 340–351](paper/rw-spheres.tex:340)). This also works when \(\|y-s\|=t\), eliminating any ball-boundary ambiguity. Thus the printed \(4k\sqrt d\) conclusion holds.

(ii) **CONFIRMED.** Take \(U\) as the open radius-\(0.01\) ball. The paper explicitly defines \(B_r(U)\) using Euclidean distance **at most** \(r\) ([lines 1432–1435](paper/rw-spheres.tex:1432)); consequently
\[
B_2(U)=\{x:\|x-s\|\le2.01\}.
\]
For \(d\ge14\), every integer point with \(g_1\ge1\) satisfies
\[
\|g-s\|\ge\sqrt{0.99^2+(d-1)/4}
\ge\sqrt{4.2301}>2.01.
\]
Hence every included grid point has \(g_1\le0\). Each tested grid center itself belongs to \(S\), so the **exact printed cube hypothesis** holds for every admissible \(\delta\).

For \(q=s+te_1\), every \(g\in S\setminus\{s\}\) satisfies
\[
\|q-g\|^2-\|q-s\|^2
=\|g-s\|^2+2t(0.01-g_1)>0.
\]
Therefore the entire ray belongs to \(\mathcal C_s\).

There is no excluded hypothesis: \(S\) is finite, hence locally finite; \(k=1\) is allowed; and the lemma imposes no relation between \(k\) and the size of \(U\) ([lines 1628–1639](paper/rw-spheres.tex:1628)). These global Voronoi cells cover \(\mathbb R^d\). The \(P_v,\mathcal G\) conventions at lines 226 and 542 do not identify the independently quantified \(S\) with that tiling’s vertices; bounded cells are separately required when introducing \(\mathcal G^{\mathrm{Vor}}(S)\), at lines 355–360.

Even imposing bounded cells would not rescue the bound: adding all integer sites outside radius \(12\sqrt d\) about \(s\) makes every cell bounded while preserving \(s+5\sqrt d\,e_1\in\mathcal C_s\).

The printed proof’s coordinate-direction witnesses at [line 1673](paper/rw-spheres.tex:1673) are unjustified: translating \(s\) to the origin shifts the fixed grid, and the hypothesis need not supply those witnesses.
