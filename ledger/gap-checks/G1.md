## Answer

The gap is real. Put \(\eta_i:=\sup_{v\in S_i'\cup\partial S_i'}\operatorname{diam}(P_v)\) and \(\Sigma_i:=\sum_{e\in\mathcal E[\overline{U_i'}]}d\,\operatorname{vol}_d(Q_e)\). Proposition 3.2 supplies only
\[
\operatorname{vol}_{d-1}(Y_i')\le \frac{3\eta_i}{A\delta_i}\Sigma_i,
\]
whereas the claimed contraction uses \(3\Sigma_i/A\). The graph-closure definition expressly includes \(\partial S_i'\). Consequently, the assertion \(f'(i+1)\le2^{-i}\) at [line 1377](paper/rw-spheres.tex:1377) is unjustified.

The proposed repairs do not resolve this:

- Trimming controls error differences, not boundary-cell diameters. A high-error vertex adjacent to a zero-error exterior vertex can survive trimming.
- The decay hypothesis controls an interior boundary vertex through **all** its incident dual polytopes; some lie outside the sum defining \(f'(i)\). It need not apply to original exterior boundary vertices.
- Keeping \(\eta_i\) also leaves the energy and gradient estimates with uncontrolled scale ratios.

The interior-only strengthening is actually false in general. In a rectangular product tiling, take the central cell \([-r,r]^2\), centered at \(0\), with neighboring centers \((\pm L,0),(0,\pm L/2)\). For \(h_C=x^2-y^2\), its discrete harmonic extension to this singleton satisfies \(h_D(0)=L^2/6\). For \(r\ll L^2\), every projected column is bad at threshold \(AM\delta\), giving measure \(2r\), while the proposed interior-only estimate gives \(O(rL/A)\), which fails for small \(L\).

I would still expect Theorem 3.7 to be true. Establishing its rate needs an additional boundary-control argument or a modified iteration; correcting “smallest” does not suffice. This check does not establish that a substantially new method is necessary.

VERDICT: CONFIRMED
