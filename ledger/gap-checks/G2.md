## Answer

**CONFIRMED.** A suitable completion makes this a counterexample to [Proposition 2.6](paper/rw-spheres.tex:824), including when \(C^2(\widehat U)\) means smooth extension to an open neighborhood.

The far sites require care. Fix \(L>4\rho\); add radius-\(L\) radial copies of the four sites, the four cardinal sites of radius \(L\), and a lattice outside \(B_{10L}\). This gives a locally finite Voronoi tiling of \(\mathcal D=\mathbb R^2\) by bounded polytopes, with every site in its cell interior. The cardinal sites bound \(P_v\) inside \(B_{2L}\), so no exterior lattice site neighbors \(v\). The radial ray through \(z\) meets only \(P_z\), its radial copy’s cell, and exterior lattice cells; none neighbors \(v\). Thus the proposed cut can avoid all of \(\widehat U\). The stated adjacencies hold, and choosing \(U\) sufficiently small gives exactly the asserted vertex closure. Relevant cells remain uniformly bounded.

For an explicit nonzero jump, write \(\zeta=x+iy\), set \(b=\rho/4\) and
\[
c_\alpha=b\bigl(\sin(\alpha/3)+i\cos(\alpha/3)\bigr),\qquad
F_\alpha(\zeta)=\frac{4}{15\sqrt R}(\zeta-c_\alpha)^{5/2}.
\]
Take the radial cut through \(c_\alpha\) and argument in
\((\pi/2-\alpha/3,\pi/2-\alpha/3+2\pi)\). A fixed sufficiently large \(R\) works for all small \(\alpha\). Then \(h_C=\operatorname{Re}F_\alpha\) is smooth and harmonic on a neighborhood of \(\widehat U\), with Hessian operator norm at most \(1\) there. Directly,
\[
J=\lim_{\alpha\to0}\bigl(h_C(w)-h_C(v)\bigr)
=\frac{4\sqrt2}{15\sqrt R}(\rho-b)^{5/2}>0.
\]
Other Hessian norm conventions introduce only a fixed factor. The same-side difference \(h_C(y)-h_C(v)\) is \(O(\alpha)\).

For this completion,
\[
\alpha\,\operatorname{cond}(v,w)\longrightarrow\frac{L-\rho}{4\rho},
\qquad
\alpha\,\operatorname{cond}(v,y)\longrightarrow\frac{3(L+\rho)}{4\rho}.
\]
All other incident conductances and boundary values stay bounded. Consequently,
\[
f(v)\longrightarrow\frac{L-\rho}{4L+2\rho}\,J\ne0,
\qquad
\mathcal D(f,U)=f(v)^2\sum_{u\sim v}\operatorname{cond}(v,u)
\asymp\alpha^{-1}.
\]
Here the paper’s edge convention includes edges incident to \(\mathcal V[U]\). Meanwhile \(\varepsilon\) stays bounded, and the dual polytopes have disjoint interiors inside uniformly bounded \(\widehat U\), so the right side stays bounded.

The missing segment control is therefore substantive. **The smallest local analytic repair I would use** is to require a \(C^2\) extension to every relevant edge segment \([v,w]\), with the same Hessian bound \(M\) there. Harmonicity is needed only on \(\widehat U\). Taylor’s theorem then applies; the gradient comparison can be taken entirely inside the convex cell \(P_w\), from \(w\) to a facet point. A geometric alternative is to require those segments to lie in \(\widehat U\).

VERDICT: CONFIRMED
